# Build 33 — Foundations Audit & Pedagogy Specification

Status: **AUDIT CANDIDATE / NO RUNTIME CHANGE**

Baseline audited: **V2.2.0 / Build 32 successor — 52 lessons / 313 items** plus the post-Build32 field-reliability hotfix. Historical V2 40/241 remains a separate frozen contract.

## 1. Audit question

French Trân’quille already teaches many communicative chunks and increasingly includes small grammar notes. The audit asks a different question:

> Does Trân receive an explicit foundation that lets her understand and generalize the mechanism, or is the mechanism only embedded implicitly in memorized phrases?

This distinction matters because recognizing `la pharmacie` is not the same evidence as being able to choose `la`, form a plural, or transfer the pattern to a new noun.

## 3. Concept audit

Legend:

- **EXPLICIT** — a contextual rule is currently explained;
- **PARTIAL** — examples exist and/or a later rule touches the concept, but the foundation itself is incomplete;
- **IMPLICIT** — Trân uses forms without a dedicated explanation;
- **MISSING FOUNDATION** — no short, reusable conceptual base currently teaches the mechanism as a transferable rule.

### F01 — noun gender: masculine / feminine

Status: **MISSING FOUNDATION / heavily implicit early**.

Early examples already used before any explicit gender explanation:

- `un café`
- `la gare`
- `la pharmacie`
- `un billet`
- `une table`
- `la carte`
- `un médecin`
- `mon fiancé / ma famille`

Later lessons indirectly depend on gender, especially possessives and adjective agreement, but there is no earlier capsule explaining that French nouns normally carry grammatical gender and should be learned with an article.

**Pilot priority: very high.**

### F02 — indefinite articles `un / une / des`

Status: **MISSING FOUNDATION / implicit early / partial later**.

Forms occur from the first survival curriculum (`un café`, `un thé`, `un billet`, `une heure`, `une table`, `un médecin`) but are not introduced as a system. Later quantity/partitive work assumes article awareness.

**Pilot priority: very high.**

### F03 — definite articles `le / la / l’ / les`

Status: **MISSING FOUNDATION / heavily implicit early**.

Early examples include:

- `l’addition`
- `les toilettes`
- `la gare`
- `la pharmacie`
- `la carte`
- `à la tête`

Build 32 later uses many article-bearing noun phrases, but there is still no compact foundation that contrasts singular gender and plural.

**Pilot priority: very high.**

### F04 — singular / plural nouns and articles

Status: **MISSING FOUNDATION / implicit**.

Plural appears early (`les toilettes`, numbers + nouns, later `mes affaires`, `tes clés`, quantities and packaging) but no foundation explicitly teaches the basic shift:

`le / la → les`, `un / une → des`, plus common written plural `-s` with the warning that final `s` is usually not pronounced.

**Pilot priority: very high.**

### F05 — subject pronouns

Status: **PARTIAL / distributed**.

The curriculum gradually introduces perspectives rather than a pronoun table:

- `je` dominates early chunks;
- `tu` is made explicit later;
- `il / elle` follows;
- `nous` follows;
- `on` is explicitly treated in lesson 52;
- `vous` appears from polite survival language.

The progression is pedagogically sound, but a later compact consolidation capsule is still useful.

**Pilot priority: medium, after F01–F04.**

### F06 — `être` present

Status: **EXPLICIT**.

Lesson 16 explicitly introduces `je suis • tu es • il/elle est • c’est`, with later reuse in states, adjectives and past forms.

Foundation work should **reuse**, not duplicate, this lesson. A later capsule can consolidate missing persons (`nous / vous / ils`) when useful.

### F07 — `avoir` present

Status: **EXPLICIT / practical subset**.

Lesson 17 explicitly teaches `j’ai • tu as`, negation and practical uses. Later lessons reinforce sensations, needs and past construction.

A foundation capsule should consolidate rather than re-teach from zero.

### F08 — regular `-er` present

Status: **PARTIAL / explicit pattern later**.

Later A1 lessons explicitly compare forms such as `je travaille → tu travailles`, and other subjects are introduced progressively. The app teaches the useful pattern but not yet one compact generalization capsule.

Priority after article/gender foundations.

### F09 — `aller` + futur proche

Status: **EXPLICIT later**.

The current A1 sequence already teaches future proche contextually. Foundation work should map to and consolidate that content, not create a parallel conflicting lesson.

### F10 — `vouloir / pouvoir / devoir`

Status: **EXPLICIT / distributed**.

`vouloir / pouvoir` are explicitly taught in Stage 2; `devoir` is introduced in practical work/consigne content. Consolidation is useful, duplication is not.

### F11 — negation

Status: **PARTIAL / recurrent but fragmented**.

Negation appears extremely early (`Je ne comprends pas`, `Je n’aime pas`) and is later explained around `avoir`, `pouvoir`, `il y a`, etc. There is no single reusable foundation connecting the pattern across verbs.

Priority: high after F01–F04.

### F12 — questions

Status: **PARTIAL / communicatively strong, system weak**.

The curriculum contains many real questions: `Où… ?`, `Quelle heure… ?`, `Combien… ?`, `Tu… ?`, `Pouvez-vous… ?`, etc. This is good communicative exposure, but a compact later foundation should help Trân see the recurring question strategies without overloading her with inversion grammar.

### F13 — adjective agreement

Status: **PARTIAL**.

The curriculum intentionally uses forms relevant to Trân (`prête`, `vietnamienne`, `fatiguée`, `inquiète`, past feminine forms). Lesson 38 explicitly explains feminine written agreement in a past context, but there is no general beginner capsule for common adjective agreement.

### F14 — possessives

Status: **EXPLICIT**.

Lesson 31 explicitly teaches `mon / ma / mes` and `ton / ta / tes` and correctly explains that the form follows the possessed noun.

Foundation work should link back to F01 gender rather than repeat lesson 31 wholesale.

### F15 — partitives / quantities

Status: **EXPLICIT / practical**.

Stage 2 explicitly teaches `du pain`, `du lait`, `des œufs`; Build 32 quantities/packaging reinforces `de` after quantity (`une bouteille d’eau`, etc.).

A later capsule may consolidate the system once F01–F04 are secure.

### F16 — `à / de` contractions

Status: **PARTIAL / distributed**.

The curriculum uses `au`, `à la`, `du`, `des`, `de l’` in many real phrases but does not yet provide one compact reusable contraction map. This is a valid Core foundation, but not the first pilot.

### F17 — recent past / passé composé

Status: **EXPLICIT later**.

Stage 3 explicitly introduces recent past, basic passé composé with `avoir`, then movement verbs with `être` and feminine written forms. Foundation work should consolidate rather than duplicate.

### F18 — spoken `on`

Status: **EXPLICIT in lesson 52**.

Build 32 deliberately teaches `on` as high-value spoken French and notes that it conjugates like `il / elle` (`on va`, `on peut`, `on doit`). No separate early pilot needed.

## 4. Main conclusion

The largest remaining foundation hole is **not conjugation in general**: several core verbs and tenses already have explicit contextual teaching from lesson 16 onward.

The clearest hole is earlier and more basic:

> **noun gender + articles + singular/plural**

Trân has already seen and used these forms dozens of times, but the app has not yet given her the conceptual key that makes them reusable outside memorized chunks.

That makes F01–F04 the safest and highest-value pilot.

## 5. Build 34 pilot specification

Pilot name: **Articles, genre & nombre**.

Scope is intentionally small and non-persistent.

### Concepts

- F01 noun gender;
- F02 `un / une / des`;
- F03 `le / la / l’ / les`;
- F04 singular / plural.

### Contextual anchors

Use only real curriculum vocabulary already encountered around lessons 8–12:

- `la gare`
- `la pharmacie`
- `les toilettes`
- `un billet`
- `une heure`
- `une table`
- `la carte`
- `un café`

No artificial vocabulary dump.

### Pedagogical sequence

1. **Observe** examples already known.
2. **Explain in Vietnamese**: learn a noun together with its article; gender is grammatical and cannot always be guessed.
3. **Construct**: choose the correct article.
4. **Contrast** singular / plural.
5. **Transfer** to one new combination built from known vocabulary.

### UX

- no new top-level Grammar tab;
- a compact `🧩 Nền tảng nhỏ / Petite base utile` entry appears contextually in lessons 8–12;
- opening it shows one focused mini-capsule;
- return goes back to the exact lesson step;
- normal learner language remains Vietnamese-heavy;
- DEBUG FR translates the explanation for Jerry;
- no giant conjugation/reference table;
- no mandatory microphone use;
- one capsule should feel like roughly 4–7 minutes, but the learner may dismiss/continue at any time.

### Evidence during pilot

**No new durable evidence schema.**

Build 34 may keep only ephemeral in-memory answers during the open capsule. It must not add a localStorage store, migrate the learner, or pretend one correct multiple choice means the rule is mastered.

The eventual Memory Evidence v2 will decide how to represent concept understanding and transfer after the pilot is validated.
